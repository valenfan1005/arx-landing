Two Assets, Same Flat Market, Opposite Crowds

BTC and ETH are both near-flat today — up 0.56% and 0.48% respectively. But their perpetual funding structures are mirror images. BTC carries positive funding at +8.3% APR, meaning long-side traders are the dominant force and paying to hold their positions. ETH carries negative funding at −11.7% APR, meaning short-side traders dominate and are paying to hold their short positions.

The cost arithmetic is straightforward. ETH’s open interest stands at $1.90B. At −11.7% APR, ETH shorts pay approximately $607K per day in funding costs — distributed to the open-interest counterparty pool via the protocol, a carry cost that compounds the longer the short position remains open without a favourable price move. BTC’s $2.26B OI at +8.3% APR means BTC longs are paying approximately $513K per day to maintain their positions. Neither crowd is getting price confirmation today.

SOL reinforces the ETH pattern: −6.5% APR funding on $329M OI, meaning SOL shorts are paying roughly $58K/day to stay short on a coin that’s down just 0.21%. Combined, ETH and SOL shorts are paying over $660K/day to maintain bearish positions in a near-flat market.

On-Chain Structural Note

When funding stays deeply negative (shorts paying longs) on a large-OI asset like ETH, the short side is carrying a compounding cost. Each day the position remains open without a favourable price move, that cost erodes the short’s effective entry. At −11.7% APR, a short entered today loses roughly 1% of notional value per month to carry alone, before any adverse price move. That does not predict a squeeze — it describes the observable cost structure. The data records what traders are paying; it does not specify why, or for how long they will continue.

Wall Street Backs the Clarity Act — While On-Chain Traders Short ETH

The same day this snapshot was taken, BlackRock, Fidelity, and Goldman Sachs announced support for the Clarity Act, per CoinDesk — the U.S. legislative framework that would define regulatory jurisdiction over digital assets and provide a clear path for institutional participation in crypto markets. BlackRock, Fidelity, and Goldman Sachs endorsing the same legislation in the same news cycle is notable; these are among the largest asset managers and banks globally.

The contrast is observable but not contradictory. TradFi firms publicly backing regulatory clarity are seeking a durable legal framework for the asset class. Whether and how such a framework would be implemented, and on what timeline, is not observable from the endorsement alone. What the data records: the largest names in traditional finance are pushing for a U.S. crypto framework while Hyperliquid’s perp market shows net-short positioning on Ethereum.

Separately, oil moved higher following CNBC reports that Iran resumed strikes after a brief ceasefire pause. The macro backdrop carries a risk-off signal. Crypto has absorbed it near flat — BTC and ETH are marginally green, SOL and HYPE slightly red. The absence of a sharp crypto decline in the face of renewed geopolitical escalation is observable, though not directionally conclusive.

What to Watch

  • ETH funding direction — whether the sustained negative funding rate at −11.7% narrows toward zero or widens further. A move toward neutral would change the carry cost structure for both sides, reducing the daily cost pressure on the short side. If funding stays deeply negative while price holds, the short side continues to pay carry without price confirmation. These are conditions to observe; direction and timing are not derivable from the current snapshot.
  • BTC carry sustainability — BTC longs at +8.3% APR on $2.26B OI are paying $515K/day to stay long near $63,671. If price stalls at this level, the daily carry cost accumulates on open positions. A break above resistance levels reduces this pressure; a break below inverts it.
  • Clarity Act momentum — endorsement from BlackRock, Fidelity, and Goldman signals serious legislative traction. Watch for Senate co-sponsors and committee movement. The framework, if passed, would set the regulatory baseline for institutional on-chain activity for years. It does not affect today’s perp funding; it affects the multi-year trajectory of assets like ETH.
  • Oil and crypto decoupling — Iran escalation pushed oil higher. Crypto held near flat. If oil continues to rise on supply-shock narratives and crypto continues to ignore it, that decoupling widens the divergence between commodity risk-off and digital-asset positioning.
